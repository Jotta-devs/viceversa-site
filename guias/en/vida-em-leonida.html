GTA VI is not only about missions. Between jobs, Jason and Lucia have to earn money, find and keep cars, stay in shape and live somewhere — and Rockstar has made each of those things a system of its own. Most of what we know comes from a long interview Rob Nelson, co-studio head at Rockstar North, gave to the Japanese magazine Famitsu in September 2026, plus the Extended Look shown in August. Here is how daily life in Leonida works, system by system.

Money: cash in your pocket, savings in the bank

For the first time in a single-player GTA, money is split in two:

  • Cash is what Jason and Lucia carry. If you're killed ("Wasted") or arrested ("Busted"), you lose all of it.
  • The bank account is safe. Depositing your earnings protects them.

This is closer to how GTA Online has worked for years and it changes the rhythm of the game: after a big score, the smart move is to get to safety and deposit the money before taking new risks. Rockstar also said that story missions alone won't pay for everything, so earning in the open world is part of progressing.

Robberies: stores, jewelers, banks and gang stashes

The open world is full of targets. Rockstar described robberies of stores, jewelry shops, small banks and gang hideouts, and said you can do them alone or as a pair. The map shows information about hideouts, including how much cash or drugs they hold, so you can plan.

Stores have security cameras and alarm systems. You can go in loud, or you can enter through a back door with a lock-picking tool, disable the cameras and alarm, and empty the register quietly. Wearing a mask and removing it afterwards helps you escape without being identified — see our wanted system guide for how that connects to the police.

Cars: stealing, scanning, registering and garages

Vehicle theft has become a progression system:

  • Older cars can be opened with basic tools. In the Extended Look, Jason uses a slim jim to open a door without breaking the glass.
  • Luxury cars have alarms and trackers and need a key cloner, which you only get later.
  • You can scan a vehicle before stealing it to see its model, value and what it would cost to register.
  • Stolen cars appear with a gray icon and disappear if you restart the game unless you make them yours.

Pay 'n' Spray is how a stolen car becomes yours: it repairs it, repaints it and registers it as your property. It only offers basic color changes — deeper customization happens at tuning shops. Some cars can't be sold right away and have to be abandoned or taken to a fence who removes the tracker. Damage lowers resale value, so careful driving pays. Early on, your garage holds three vehicles; buying more garages increases the space.

Rockstar also said roads are narrower and more realistic than before, so traffic jams are part of city driving — another reason to plan your getaway route.

Fitness: gyms, stats and body shape

GTA San Andreas fans will recognize this one. GTA VI tracks each character's health, stamina and physical condition, and the way you play changes their bodies: training builds muscle, while neglecting it can lead to weight gain. Gyms return as full minigames. According to Rockstar, leveling up takes a routine — three exercises a day over three consecutive days — managing stamina as you go. The Extended Look also showed weight training among the side activities.

Food: temporary boosts

Eating gives temporary bonuses to health, stamina and endurance on top of your base values, which you raise through training. Rockstar noted that a health bonus from food disappears if your actual health drops below its maximum, so it's a buffer, not a shield.

Hideouts that change with the story

The story is told in chapters, and each chapter can move Jason and Lucia to a new hideout — similar to how the gang's camp moved in Red Dead Redemption 2. It all starts in the Leonida Keys, at Jason's first house. Rockstar says hideouts are far more detailed than safehouses in earlier GTAs: beds with animated covers, fridges that fill up naturally, TVs with ads and characters who follow daily routines. The Extended Look showed Jason and Lucia relaxing at home, picking drinks from the fridge and watching TV.

Some hideouts offer basic haircut options, while salons around the map have many more styles. Clothing stores are spread across the state, and Rockstar mentioned thousands of clothing options; the Extended Look also showed a quick-change wheel for outfits and accessories.

The phone

The phone is now a physical object in Jason's and Lucia's hands rather than a flat overlay. Rockstar listed apps for video streaming, fitness tracking, banking, social media and "Scooter Bros", a vehicle delivery service. Because the couple is laying low, they can't post on social media, but they can follow other people's accounts and get notified when someone nearby is livestreaming. The two can also message each other and share photos.

Side activities

The Extended Look showed a long list of things to do between jobs: street and track racing, jet skis, scuba diving, basketball, dirt bikes and the gym, plus NPC interactions in the style of Red Dead Redemption 2, where holding a trigger lets you choose what to say to people around you. Rockstar hasn't published a full list yet.

Frequently asked questions

Can you buy houses in GTA 6?

Rockstar has talked about hideouts that change with the story and about buying extra garages. It hasn't detailed buying homes freely.

Do you lose money when you die in GTA 6?

You lose the cash you're carrying. Money in the bank is safe.

Is there a gym in GTA 6?

Yes. Training changes your character's body and raises base stats.
